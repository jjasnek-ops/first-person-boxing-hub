## What combat mechanics are confirmed?

The official description identifies punching, slipping, countering, different guards for head and body, stamina management, and cinematic knockdowns and replays. It also lists AI practice and fights against other players. Those features form this reference; the developer description does not provide a complete ruleset or numerical balance data.

**Checked:** 15 October 2026. This is a source-based guide. We have not entered a live session or measured the mechanics ourselves.

## How should you think about head and body guards?

Use the distinction between head and body protection as the first thing to observe. The developer explicitly advertises different guards for those areas. A sensible practice task is to identify the target area of an incoming attack and compare what happens with each available guard, using the inputs actually shown in your session.

Do not assume holding one defense protects every target. Equally, the description alone does not prove a specific guard-break rule. If an attack gets through, check your input, target area and timing before deciding the game has a hidden mechanic.

Our drill: spend several exchanges identifying attack height before adding a punch. This is a learning suggestion, not a tested defensive exploit.

## How do slips and counters fit together?

Slips and counters are both confirmed, but we have not established that every successful slip guarantees a counter, or that counters require a slip. Treat them as two mechanics to observe. Learn to perform the slip, then test whether a short follow-up attack connects under the same conditions.

Keep your comparisons simple:

1. Observe an attack and try your current slip input.
2. Check whether the attack connected or missed.
3. Try a single follow-up punch on the next similar exchange.
4. Compare the visible outcome, then reset.

We do not publish a millisecond window, a fixed damage bonus or a guaranteed combo. A replay can be useful for reviewing an exchange if the game offers it in that situation; the official description confirms replays but does not document when every replay appears.

## How should you manage stamina?

Pay attention to tiredness before you commit to repeated attacks. The developer says it slows you down, so a useful practice comparison is a short attack sequence against a longer one. Watch how your movement and next action feel, then leave enough room to observe recovery rather than pressing constantly.

| What you notice | An editorial practice adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| Actions feel slower after repeated offense | Shorten the next sequence and compare |
| You cannot tell which action spent stamina | Test one action repeatedly in AI practice |
| A long exchange becomes hard to read | Reset your pace and focus on one defensive decision |
| You miss repeatedly | Check distance before increasing attack volume |

These adjustments are general learning methods. We have not confirmed that every action spends stamina, that blocking consumes it, or that a particular pause guarantees full recovery.

## What should you test before trusting a combo?

A clip of a successful sequence does not establish a guaranteed combination. To evaluate one, check that it is the same Roblox experience and current input scheme, then see whether the opponent can defend, move or interrupt between attacks. Repeatability matters more than a single dramatic knockout.

We have not verified named punch types, unlockable fighting styles, glove statistics or damage scaling. Those systems are excluded until reliable evidence is available. Advice for a different Roblox boxing game should not be transferred without testing.
