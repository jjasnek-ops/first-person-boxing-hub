## How should you start First Person Boxing?

Open the [official Roblox experience](https://www.roblox.com/games/132956556682909/First-Person-Boxing), confirm the creator is **Can You Box?**, and use AI practice to learn one action at a time. The developer confirms both AI practice and player fights. The steps below are a learning plan, not a mandatory tutorial or a progression route.

**Scope:** checked against the official description on 15 October 2026. We have not played a game session, verified menu paths, or tested inputs. A game update can change the interface without changing that description.

## What should you check before your first round?

Identify the action prompts on your own device before trying a combination. Make sure you can distinguish movement, punching, the different guards and slipping. We do not yet have a verified keyboard or touch layout, so a specific key from another boxing game could teach you the wrong action.

## How can you learn defense without getting overwhelmed?

![An original three-step diagram: observe one input, practice against AI, review the outcome.](/images/practice-loop.svg)

Our suggested first drill is to separate head defense from body defense. The official description confirms different guards for these areas. In AI practice, focus on recognizing where an attack is directed and using the corresponding guard, rather than trying to block, slip and counter in every exchange.

1. Find the current guard inputs.
2. Observe which attacks are directed toward your head or body.
3. Try the relevant guard and watch the visible result.
4. Reset and repeat before adding another action.

This is an observation exercise. We have not measured damage reduction, whether a particular attack bypasses a guard, or whether there is a separate perfect-block window. See the [combat reference](/guides/combat/) for the boundary between confirmed mechanics and advice.

## When should you add slips and counters?

After you can identify the guard actions, practice slipping as a separate task. The developer describes slips and powerful counters, but does not publish an exact timing window. Watch the attack, try the slip input shown in your game, and compare the outcome before attempting a follow-up punch.

For a simple progression, practice one defensive action first, then try one punch after a successful defensive exchange. Return to a neutral situation and repeat. This drill is intended to reduce the number of things you are tracking; it does not claim a guaranteed combo or an invulnerable sequence.

Do not judge the whole mechanic from one miss. Distance, your input timing and the opponent's action may differ. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what helped.

## Why should you watch stamina early?

Learn the relationship between your action choices and tiredness before trying long strings of attacks. The official description says getting tired slows you down. That makes stamina an early learning priority, even though recovery rates, individual action costs and exhaustion thresholds have not been confirmed by this site.

Try a short sequence, pause, and observe the interface and movement. Compare it with a longer sequence. The useful question is whether your next defensive action remains comfortable, not how many punches you can press in a row. Read [stamina and combat fundamentals](/guides/combat/#how-should-you-manage-stamina) for a more focused drill.

## How do you move from practice into player fights?

Choose one goal for your next player fight, such as recognizing body attacks or avoiding unnecessary punches. A narrow goal makes it easier to learn from the result. Wins are useful feedback, but they are not the only way to tell whether your inputs and decisions are becoming more consistent.

After the match, write down one recurring problem and take it back into AI practice. Avoid changing your whole approach after a single knockout. We have not verified ranked rules, matchmaking systems, currencies or stat upgrades, so this guide does not prescribe a grind or a build.
